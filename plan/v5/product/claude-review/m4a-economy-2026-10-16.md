Reviewer: Claude Design Reviewer (advisory only, no CSV or spec edited). Pass ID `m4a-econ-2026-10-16`.

That wrapper passes `--help` as a prompt, and the call hung and was killed at 30 s. Call 2 went through `ops/claude/claude-design.sh`, hung more than 13 min with no output, and was killed. **Every finding below comes from my own scripts.** No independent model has cross-checked them.

**Scripts** (all under `claude-review/scratch/`): `m4a_review.py` → `m4a_review_out.txt` (§1 ROI, §2 waits, §3 migration sweep, §4 stack); `mig_edges.py` → `mig_edges_out.txt`.
**Model:** a deterministic flow model that reads only `economy.f1-m4cols.csv`.
- Income/s = min(eggs_per_sec[H] × Hens milestone, Farm demand) × v7 price.
- Farm demand = mean(demand_min, demand_max)[F] / customer_interval_s[F].
- Not modelled: tap-collection limits, workers, RNG.

It is cruder than Economy's `m4_active.py` and PM Farm's `tr5_run.py`, so treat the absolute times as indicative. The structural findings (thresholds, caps, sweeps) don't depend on that.

## Blocker
**B1. The TR-4 "never-lower price" rule fails for reachable saves that have a high Collection level (M4A-28, spec line 172).**
- v6 price = egg_price[F] × (1 + collection_price_bonus[C]). This reproduces all three of Economy's fixtures exactly: $2.76 / $4.27 / $6.08 (m4a_review_out §3).
- The v7 price at the Egg value cap, min(F+3, 30), cannot reach the v6 price in **186 of 961 (F, C) saves** (§3). The failures start at C ≥ F+5 for F0–F1, C ≥ F+7 for F2–F4, C ≥ F+9 at F5, and they continue up to F13 (C ≥ 30) (mig_edges_out).
- These saves are reachable. In the CSV, collection_cost < farm_cost at every level L0–L10, so a player who taps a lot can plausibly sit at C well above F in the early game.
- M4A-28 says to cap V at F+3, but it says nothing about what happens when that cap is still below the v6 price. Whether the save keeps a "price floor", lifts the cap, or gets refunded is unspecified. QA fixtures use C = F−3 only (line 164), so they never hit this case.
- **Needs a rule from HoP/Economy before the build.** For example: store a per-save price floor, or allow V up to the lowest level that matches v6 even beyond F+3.

## Major
**M1. Hens has almost no marginal value from F10 to F30 in the flow model (track dominance, Q1).**
- From H10, the ×1.25 milestone (CSV hens_ms1) pushes supply above Farm demand: at F10 it is 13.75 vs 13.38 eggs/s, and at F20 31.5 vs 25.7 (§1).
- From that point, ROI(H) is 0 at every F10–F29 when H = F, and Egg value is the best buy at every F from 10 to 29. Hens is the best buy only at F5–F9 (§1 table).
- The ×1.5 at H20 and ×2.0 at H30 therefore add nothing to in-tab income. They only fill the coop and raise offline eggs (M4A-4).
- **My view differs from Economy's here.** Economy describes the Hens milestones as "demand mostly binds that" (m4-active-checks line 252), but still lists Hens as a balanced track. PM Farm's no-dominance AC M4A-39 passes only because Balanced (min(F,H,V)) and the cap force Hens purchases, not because Hens pays back.
- The real tradeoff from F10 is Farm vs Egg value, with Hens acting as a Balanced tax.
- **Suggest** checking it with `tr5_run.py` (does the greedy bot buy Hens after H10 only to reach Balanced thresholds?) and considering Hens milestones that raise demand or coop capacity.

**M2. First-minute income goes over 1.5× v6 at non-fixture saves (Q3).**
- At the spec fixtures I get static ratios of 1.33 / 1.35 / 1.32 (§3). That is consistent with Economy's measured 1.31–1.37 (spec line 169).
- But **46 of 961 saves exceed 1.5×**, with a peak of **1.66× at F12 / C1** (V0 = 5, $1.79 → $2.42) (§3).
- Two save types cause it (mig_edges_out):
  - (a) Low-Collection saves (C0–C2) at F10–F19. V0 lands just over a grade or Balanced step: V5 gives Grade A ×1.1 and also turns on Balanced tier 1 ×1.1 at once, because min(F, F, V0) = 5.
  - (b) High-Collection late saves (F22–F30, C ≥ F+4): V0 = 20 unlocks Jumbo ×1.5.
- The step functions make "lowest V with price ≥ v6" overshoot by up to one grade times one Balanced tier. There is also a supply part from F10: H = F gets the ×1.25 milestone for free.
- **Suggest** adding fixtures at F12/C1, F16/C0 and F23/C29, or defining the cap as a price ratio instead of a level rule.
- Caveat: this is a static ratio. Economy's dynamic sim would add the 2–4 catch-up buys in the first minute (m4-active-checks line 274), so real ratios are probably higher, not lower.

**M3. Waits over 3 min occur after minute 30 (Q2), and nothing tests for them.**
- Flow model results (§2):
  - Greedy: F18 at 18.4 min and F22 at 30 min. Both match TR-5's 18.88 / L22, which is a sanity check that the model is in range.
  - **No wait over 180 s before about F23.**
  - After that the greedy buyer waits 184–280 s at F24–F26. Casual (demand ×0.75, cheapest-first) waits up to 196 s at F23. Greedy at demand ×0.75 waits up to 256 s.
- TR-5 criterion 3 (line 230) only covers the first 30:00 run, so these L23–L30 dead zones are never gated. The brief asked about L1–L30. (Workers and the Grader, which my model leaves out, would shorten these waits. They need a check in `tr5_run.py`.)

**M4. The full multiplier stack is ×7.8 on price, not ×6.0 (Q4).**
- Jumbo ×1.5 × Balanced5 ×1.3 × Grader T5 ×4.0 (w4_price_mult, CSV L24+) = **×7.8** (§4). M4A-14's example (line 99) gives ×6.0 because it leaves Balanced out.
- At F25 the price is $17.55 without the Grader and $70.20 with it (§4).
- **Runaway stacking:** none. Each family is "highest tier only", there's no compounding, and the Hens milestones go to waste above demand (M1).
- **Active/idle:** every track multiplier applies to active and idle sales alike, so the tracks can't move the in-tab ratio (I agree with m4-active-checks line 29: 1.00). The ≤2× gate therefore depends on the rest-of-M4 mechanics, which are out of M4-A's scope (spec line 43).
- One caveat: offline excludes the Grader (m4-active-checks line 94), so the active-to-offline gap grows with the ×7.8 stack.
- **No exploit found** in Farm-lagging. +1 Farm level is always worth it when H and V are at the cap: +5.4 to +54.9 $/s (§4).
