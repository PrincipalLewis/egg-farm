# Critic review: rc1-pt1 (build 2490c79), Oct 16 2026

**Score: 5.5 / 10**

Session: ~30 min of play, Farm L0 to L17 of 30. Build served locally from the release asset `egg-farm-rc1-pt1-2490c79.html`. Some play was driven by short repeated loops, so tap-feel notes are partial. Audio was not evaluated. Screenshots are in `shots/` (01-14).

## First impressions and onboarding
The game looks clean and readable, and the tutorial gets you to your first purchase in about 90 seconds (Collect, Sell, Buy, then the goal bubble). That is better than most idlers at this stage. Two snags:
- The first ETA you see is "Farm L1 $100: Affordable in ~6:20". It's based on the idle rate, but active tapping gets there in about 4 minutes. A 6-minute wait in minute one is a discouraging first number.
- The tutorial says "Tap **Manage** to perk them up!" but the button reads **Refill** (shot 04).

## Core loop
Up to about L7 the loop is fun and tactile: tap, sell, and something new to buy every 20-40 seconds. Once Market Hand (L3) and Coop Hand (L7) are hired, manual taps stop mattering. At L10 a Collect tap was worth about 1.5 seconds of passive income. The only real choice left is Collection upgrade versus Farm level.

## "Affordable in ~M:SS" ETA label
- It's accurate while income is steady (for example, L5: $253 at $12.22/s showed 0:16, which matches).
- It goes wrong after income spikes. Net income in the top bar is a lagging average, so after a Refill burst the L16 ETA showed 1:38 but took about 2:09. For about 50 seconds the countdown held still or went up (1:11 → 1:17 → 1:10; shot 13). A countdown that goes up erodes trust quickly.
- The cards don't agree: Collection shows "Need $X more" and Farm shows an ETA. The goal bubble sometimes disagrees with the card ("Need $1,978 more" next to "~7 s"), and it usually points at a cheap Collection upgrade, not the Farm level the player actually cares about.
- It's tiny grey text under the button and easy to miss.
- On the plus side, the idea works. When the label is right, it's one of the clearest goal signals in the build.

## Refill (formerly Manage)
- It's free and only becomes active when a worker drops below about 40%. One press puts every worker on a shared 30-second cooldown. Energy lasts about 15 minutes.
- Between 1% and 40% energy there was no measurable income loss. At 0% ("Asleep 💤") the Coop Hand stops completely: coins went flat or slightly down from wages, while the HUD still showed +$26-67/s (shot 12). The only alert is a small "Refill tired (2)" pill.
- There are three Refill buttons on screen at once: in the header, at the top of the Workers tab, and on each card. Wage units are mixed ($/s versus $/min).
- Critic's take: Refill is maintenance with no decision in it. It's invisible until it suddenly matters, and then the HUD hides that it matters. Compare Idle Miner Tycoon, where managers are a choice with tradeoffs, or Egg Inc., where your attention goes to boosts and research rather than upkeep.

## Mid game, L8-17: does it play itself?
Yes, I agree with Vadim. From about L8 the only inputs are Buy and an occasional Refill, and tapping adds 1-2%. Waits between Farm levels grow from 45-75 s (L8-13) to about 1:47 (L14), about 2:10 (L16) and 3:00+ (L17). There were dead zones at roughly 20-22, 25-28 and 28+ minutes with nothing to do but watch. Upgrades get thin (L15 gave "+5 hens, +$0.13 egg price, orders 30-59 → 30-60"). The customer queue fills up, but workers serve it automatically, so it never becomes a decision. Cookie Clicker fills this stretch with golden cookies and achievements, AdVenture Capitalist with milestone unlocks and managers, and Egg Inc. with drones, research and boosts. This build has nothing in that slot yet.

## UI, feel and juice
Strengths: the art changes at L5 (barn) and L10 (farmhouse/silo) are satisfying beats, the hen count visibly grows, and "+$" popups and order progress bars give good feedback. Observed rough edges, noted for Product to triage:
- A "+$101.40" popup stuck on screen for minutes (shots 07, 09).
- "Sell 22" order buttons active while the basket showed 0 eggs.
- The HUD briefly showed +$134.61/s while the accessibility text said $34.61.
- Grammar: "1 more customers", "Refill 1 tired workers".

## What would make me stop playing
Around minute 20: multi-minute waits with no choices, income and ETA numbers I stop trusting after a Refill, and a fail state (sleeping workers) that the HUD hides.

## Top 3 strengths
1. Fast, clear onboarding: first purchase in about 90 s, and the goal bubble is a good idea.
2. Satisfying visual progression (coop to barn to farmhouse, more hens on screen).
3. The early loop (L0-7) feels active and rewarding, with a purchase every 20-40 s.

## Top 3 problems
1. The L8+ mid game plays itself: no active decisions, and waits stretch to 3+ minutes.
2. The ETA and income readouts become unreliable after spikes. The countdown can go up, and labels and bubble disagree.
3. Refill is a decision-free chore with a hidden fail state, and the HUD shows positive income while a sleeping worker has stopped production.

## Score rationale
5.5/10. A polished, readable first 10 minutes that compares well with the genre's openings. But past L8 there is no mid-game hook, the numbers the player relies on drift, and the one new active system (Refill) is upkeep, not play. The genre leaders keep you busy exactly where this build goes quiet.
